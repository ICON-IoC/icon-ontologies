ICON Infrastructure ontology

Places and structures on the landscape that conservation works on or around: waters (blue), conservation lands (green), water works, energy, food production and sensors. Covers their types, ownership and management, and how they lie relative to one another (upstream, adjacent, intersecting).

TermKindIDDefinition
infrastructure class ICON:0005000 A place or structure on the landscape that conservation works on or around: a water body, a conservation area, a water or energy work, a farm, a sensor, a road or trail. Called infrastructure in the PPOD model whether built or natural.
blue infrastructure class ICON:0005001 A body of water: a river or stream, a lake or pond, a dry lake or playa, a wetland, an estuary or the ocean.
green infrastructure class ICON:0005002 Land or water held or designed for wildlife and habitat: conservation areas, mitigation banks, greenbelts, corridors and crossing structures.
water infrastructure class ICON:0005003 A work that stores, moves, controls or recharges water.
energy infrastructure class ICON:0005004 A work that generates or carries electric power.
food production infrastructure class ICON:0005005 Land or facilities used to grow crops or raise animals.
sensor infrastructure class ICON:0005006 A device placed in the landscape to record what passes or happens there.
transport infrastructure class ICON:0005007 A route that carries people, vehicles or goods across the landscape: a trail, a highway, a local road, a railway or a bridge.
waste infrastructure class ICON:0005009 A facility that receives, stores or disposes of solid waste, such as a landfill.
estuary class ENVO:00000045 A semi-enclosed coastal body of water with one or more rivers or streams flowing into it, and with a free connection to the open sea.
lentic water body class ENVO:01000617 A lentic water body is a water body in which the accumulated water, in its totality, has very little to no directed flow.
dry lake class ENVO:00000277 A lake that has either permanently or temporally lost its water.
marine water body class ENVO:00001999 A lentic water body which is composed primarily of marine water.
stream class ENVO:00000023 A watercourse which is linear and flows across the solid portion of a planetary surface.
wetland area class ENVO:00000043 An vegetated area which overlaps a wetland ecosystem.
canal class ENVO:00000014 Artificial watercourse with no flow or a controlled flow used for navigation, drainage or irrigation.
floodway class ENVO:00000256 The stream channel and adjacent areas that carry flood flows in a flood plain.
levee class ENVO:00000178 An artificial slope or wall, usually earthen, which parallels the course of a river, built for the purpose of containing the river.
reservoir class ENVO:00000025 An artificial body of water, often contained by a dam, constructed for the purpose of water storage.
weir class ENVO:00000535 A low obstruction built across the path of s stream to raise its level.
geothermal power plant class ENVO:00002215 A geothermal power plant is a power plant which uses heat generated by the decay of radioactive isotopes in the Earth's mantle and crust as well as primordial heat left over from the formation of the Earth to drive its generators.
power plant class ENVO:00002214 A power plant is a building which contains one or more generators which convert mechanical energy into electrical energy through the relative motion between a magnetic field and a conductor. The energy source harnessed to turn the generator varies widely.
ecological corridor class ENVO:01000930 An ecosystem which bridges two or more adjoining ecosystems and through which organisms may move or propagate.
groundwater basin class ICON:0005020 An aquifer or group of aquifers managed as a unit, such as a basin or subbasin of California's Bulletin 118.
water pipeline class ICON:0005021 A pipe or aqueduct that carries water over a distance.
pumping station class ICON:0005022 A facility that lifts or moves water with pumps.
groundwater recharge facility class ICON:0005023 Basins or works that put surface water into the ground to refill an aquifer.
water retention structure class ICON:0005024 A structure that holds water back, such as a dam, a check dam or a detention basin.
natural gas-fired power plant class ICON:0005025 A power plant that burns natural gas.
hydroelectric powerhouse class ICON:0005026 The building at a dam or penstock that holds the turbines and generators.
pumped storage facility class ICON:0005027 A hydroelectric plant that pumps water uphill when power is cheap and releases it through turbines when power is needed.
solar power plant class ICON:0005028 A power plant that makes electricity from sunlight.
wind power plant class ICON:0005029 A group of wind turbines that makes electricity.
electric transmission line class ICON:0005030 A high-voltage power line carrying electricity from plants to substations.
electrical substation class ICON:0005031 A facility where transmission lines meet and voltage is changed.
natural gas compressor station class ICON:0005053 A facility on a natural gas transmission pipeline that raises the gas pressure to keep it moving along the line. Example: San Diego Gas & Electric's Moreno Compressor Station in Moreno Valley, which boosts the gas supply into San Diego County.
animal production site class ICON:0005032 A ranch, dairy, feedlot or other place where animals are raised.
crop production site class ICON:0005033 A farm, orchard, vineyard or field where crops are grown.
conservation easement area class ICON:0005034 Land protected by a conservation easement while its owner keeps title.
fee-title conservation area class ICON:0005035 Land owned outright by an agency or organization for conservation, such as a reserve, refuge, park or wildlife area.
marine conservation area class ICON:0005036 Ocean or bay waters protected for conservation, such as a marine protected area or sanctuary.
conservation area under several protections class ICON:0005037 A conservation area made of parts held by fee title, easement or other protections together.
greenbelt class ICON:0005038 Open land kept undeveloped around or between towns.
mitigation bank class ICON:0005039 Land restored or protected so that credits can be sold to offset habitat loss elsewhere.
wildlife crossing structure class ICON:0005040 An overpass, underpass, culvert or bridge built or adapted so that animals can cross a road or railway.
camera trap class ICON:0005041 A camera set off by motion or heat that photographs animals passing.
field station class ICON:0005042 A research station or reserve that a university or agency runs for research and teaching, often with housing and labs, giving access to protected lands it may not own.
trail class ICON:0005043 A path for walking, cycling or riding, such as a regional trail like the Bay Area Ridge Trail or a preserve's trail network.
highway class ICON:0005044 A road in the state or federal highway system: an Interstate, a U.S. route or a California state route.
local road class ICON:0005045 A road a county or city owns and maintains, outside the state and federal highway systems.
railway class ICON:0005046 A rail line for freight or passenger trains.
bridge class ICON:0005057 A structure that carries a road, trail or railway over a creek, river or other obstacle. Example: the Pope-Chaucer Bridge over San Francisquito Creek between Menlo Park and Palo Alto.
GAP status class ICON:0005008 How a protected place is managed for biodiversity on the USGS Gap Analysis Project scale, as California's 30x30 Conserved Areas rate it; only GAP 1 and 2 count toward 30x30 (#427).
fish hatchery class ICON:0005049 A facility that spawns and rears fish, mostly salmon and trout, for release into rivers and lakes to supply fisheries or to make up for spawning habitat a dam has blocked.
native plant nursery class ICON:0005050 A farm or nursery that grows native plants and their seed from local wild sources for habitat restoration, rather than for sale or food.
marine conservation priority area class ICON:0005051 An area of ocean, estuary or shoreline that a conservation plan marks as a priority for protecting marine biodiversity, without itself being protected, such as a portfolio conservation area of a marine ecoregional assessment.
important bird area class ICON:0005052 A site that BirdLife International's Important Bird Areas program, run in the United States by the National Audubon Society, identifies as essential for birds because it holds species of conservation concern, range-restricted species or large concentrations of birds, without itself being protected.
important plant area class ICON:0005124 An area that the California Native Plant Society's Important Plant Areas Initiative identifies as among the most important places in the state for rare plants and rare vegetation types, without itself being protected. Example: a Tier 1 area of the Northwestern California ecoregion around the Mendocino pygmy forest, home to 37 California Rare Plant Rank 1B species.
named landform class ICON:0005054 A named natural feature of the land surface, such as a range of hills, a ridge or a valley, that a conservation plan treats as an area to protect or work in, without itself being protected. Example: the Dunnigan Hills of Yolo County, where the Yolo HCP/NCCP protects grassland for California tiger salamander.
water treatment plant class ICON:0005055 A facility that treats raw water from reservoirs, rivers or wells to drinking water standards before it enters a distribution system. Example: the San Francisco Public Utilities Commission's Sunol Valley Water Treatment Plant, which treats water from San Antonio and Calaveras reservoirs.
wastewater treatment plant class ICON:0005059 A facility that treats sewage and other wastewater before it is discharged or reused, for example as recycled water for irrigation. Example: the City of Santa Rosa's Laguna Treatment Plant, on the floodplain of the Laguna de Santa Rosa.
landfill class ICON:0005056 A site where solid waste is buried in the ground, usually in lined cells that are covered and capped when full. Example: the Sanitation Districts of Los Angeles County's Puente Hills Landfill, which closed in 2013.
species recovery area class ICON:0005058 An area that a federal or state recovery plan designates as essential to the recovery of listed species, such as a core area whose habitat the plan says to protect, without itself being protected. Example: the Mather Core Recovery Area of the 2005 Recovery Plan for Vernal Pool Ecosystems of California and Southern Oregon, where the South Sacramento HCP must mitigate vernal pool losses.
national heritage area class ICON:0005125 A region that the United States Congress designates for its nationally important natural, cultural, and historic resources, managed through partnerships led by a local coordinating entity under a management plan approved by the Secretary of the Interior, without federal ownership or land use regulation. Example: the Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta National Heritage Area, designated in 2019, whose local coordinating entity is the Delta Protection Commission.
has infrastructure type relation ICON:0005100 Links a place to one of the infrastructure type classes it belongs to. The explorer's way of stating the place's class.
is infrastructure type of relation ICON:0005101 The reverse of has infrastructure type.
owned by relation ICON:0005102 The organization holding legal title to the place.
owns relation ICON:0005103 The reverse of owned by.
managed by relation ICON:0005104 The organization that operates or manages the place, whether or not it owns it.
manages relation ICON:0005105 The reverse of managed by.
is upstream of relation ICON:0005106 Water flows from this place to the other, directly or through places between: Lake Berryessa is upstream of Monticello Dam.
is downstream of relation ICON:0005107 The reverse of is upstream of.
is adjacent to relation ICON:0005108 Two places share a boundary. Symmetric, unlike the Relation Ontology's adjacent to, which it narrows.
is habitat for relation ICON:0005109 The place supports a population of the taxon.
is found in relation ICON:0005110 The taxon lives in or uses the place: the reverse of is habitat for.
built for relation ICON:0005111 The structure was built or adapted so that the taxon can get past a barrier, as a wildlife crossing is built for the animals it lets cross a road. Unlike is habitat for, it says nothing about the taxon living there.
has crossing relation ICON:0005112 The taxon has a structure built or adapted to let it get past a barrier: the reverse of built for.
crosses relation ICON:0005117 The structure carries animals under or over the road or railway, as a wildlife crossing structure crosses the highway it passes beneath.
crossed by relation ICON:0005118 The road or railway has a structure that carries animals under or over it: the reverse of crosses.
monitors relation ICON:0005113 The sensor or array was set up to watch the taxon, as a camera trap study of fishers monitors the fisher. It says nothing about whether the taxon was found.
monitored by relation ICON:0005114 The taxon is watched by a sensor or array set up for it: the reverse of monitors.
detected relation ICON:0005115 The sensor or array recorded the taxon, as a camera trap photographs a bobcat. Unlike is habitat for, it says only that the taxon passed the sensor.
detected by relation ICON:0005116 The taxon was recorded by a sensor or array: the reverse of detected.
exchanges water with relation ICON:0005119 Water flows between the two places in either direction, as through an intertie that pumps both ways: the Cross Valley Canal exchanges water with the California Aqueduct. Unlike is upstream of it does not chain, so what lies upstream of one is not taken to lie upstream of the other.
spills into relation ICON:0005120 Some of this place's water leaves for the other at one point, as a river spills over a weir into a flood bypass, while the place as a whole may lie downstream of the other too: the Sacramento River spills into the Yolo Bypass, which rejoins it lower down. Unlike is upstream of it does not chain, so the river's tributaries are not taken to lie upstream of the bypass.
receives spill from relation ICON:0005121 The reverse of spills into.
has GAP status relation ICON:0005122 The GAP status of the place's lands, as the 30x30 Conserved Areas rate them; a place with lands at both levels has both.
is GAP status of relation ICON:0005123 The reverse of has GAP status.
capacity attribute ICON:0005200 How much the place holds or delivers, written with its unit: a reservoir's storage in acre-feet, a plant's output in megawatts.
GAP 1 value ICON:0005047 Permanently protected and managed for biodiversity, with natural disturbance such as fire or flooding allowed to run its course.
GAP 2 value ICON:0005048 Permanently protected and managed for biodiversity, with natural disturbance suppressed.
